## Project Overview

In semi-arid agricultural regions, intensive groundwater pumping for crop production places an immense strain on aquifer supplies while driving up energy consumption and agricultural carbon emissions. This research project investigates the close coupling between **groundwater dynamics**, **irrigation application efficiency**, **pumping energy demands**, and **greenhouse gas (GHG) footprints**.

By analyzing field-level operational data and regional hydro-geological trends across intensive Paddy-Wheat cropping systems, this work quantifies the carbon mitigation potential of adopting improved irrigation techniques (e.g., drip, micro-sprinklers, precision leveling) and energy-efficient pumping systems.

---

## Key Research Highlights & Findings

* **Energy & Carbon Footprint Quantification:** Evaluated the indirect carbon emissions ($CO_2$ equivalents) associated with agricultural groundwater pumping and fuel/electricity consumption.
* **Irrigation Efficiency Assessment:** Demonstrated how upgrading irrigation application methods reduces spatial water overdraft while significantly lowering pumping hours and energy usage.
* **Aquifer & Policy Coupling:** Modeled the impacts of declining water tables on future energy demands, providing policy-relevant metrics for sustainable groundwater abstraction and renewable energy integration (e.g., solar pumping).
* **Stakeholder & Community Adoption:** Analyzed socio-economic drivers and field-level challenges to foster producer adoption of energy-saving irrigation technologies.

## Methodologies & Technical Tools

* **Energy & Carbon Accounting:** Carbon footprint modeling of agricultural inputs, pump-set performance metrics, and energy conversion dynamics.
* **Hydrological & Irrigation Tools:** CropWAT, AquaCrop, Water Table Depth Trend Analysis
* **Statistical & Field Surveys:** SPSS, R, Multi-Criteria Decision Frameworks, Field Survey Datasets
