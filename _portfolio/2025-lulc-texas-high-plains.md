## Project Overview

The Texas High Plains is a critical agricultural hub facing significant environmental pressure due to groundwater depletion, shifting precipitation patterns, and changing land management practices. 

This research project evaluates historical spatiotemporal dynamics, quantifies land use and land cover (LULC) transitions over recent decades, and models future land cover predictions across the region. The findings provide crucial insights for regional groundwater conservation, sustainable agricultural planning, and climate-resilient ecosystem management.

---

## Key Highlights & Findings

* **Spatiotemporal Mapping:** Multi-decadal tracking of land cover changes across croplands, grasslands, and developed areas using high-resolution satellite imagery.
* **Predictive Modeling:** Simulated future LULC scenarios to anticipate land conversion trends and their potential impact on regional water resources.
* **Resource Management Insights:** Highlighted the direct link between shifting agricultural land management and long-term groundwater sustainability in the Ogallala Aquifer region.
